Your group works to close the gap in energy efficiency between computers and the brain. The closest thing I have done is making a model small enough to run on weak hardware. On the device's own microphone the false alarms tripled, so I also learned that a model has to be tested on the hardware it will run on. Retraining cut false activations from 60 to 3 in a 388-hour test. My thesis led to an ICBME 2026 paper (IEEE) and two more submissions.

I have not designed circuits or worked on memory devices, and I would be learning that side. I would start from the machine learning side of associative memories, studying how much precision a Hopfield-style model needs before its recall breaks, since that sets what the hardware has to provide.
