I am applying for the doctoral position in wearable biofeedback systems for cardiovascular health in the Intelligent Heart Technology Lab. I completed the requirements for my MSc in Biomedical Engineering, with a bioelectric specialization, at Sharif University of Technology on 23 September 2026.

The advert lists bio-signal processing, system modelling, embedded systems and physiology, and it asks for validation on human participants. The embedded and signal processing parts are what I have done. My thesis was an always-on voice assistant for older adults. I designed a 75k-parameter int8 model that runs in real time on a Raspberry Pi, with a streaming front end written in C++ and checked against the Python version to within one millionth. Offline scores looked good, but on the device's own sensor the false alarms tripled. Retraining on data recorded on the device cut false activations from 60 to 3 in a 388-hour test. I learned to separate the effect of the signal from the effect of the model, and to test on people the model has never seen. My thesis led to an ICBME 2026 paper (IEEE) and two more submissions.

I have not worked with cardiovascular recordings yet. I would start with a small model for one wearable signal that runs on the device, tested on a split where whole participants are held out, because people differ and a wearable has to work for the next person.
