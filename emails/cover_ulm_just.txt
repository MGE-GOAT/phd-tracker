Dear Prof. Just,

I am applying for the doctoral position on the wearable knee orthosis for personalized rehabilitation at the Institute of Biomedical Engineering. I am finishing my MSc in Biomedical Engineering at Sharif University of Technology. My thesis was a health device that has to work every day in people's homes, which is the problem your project describes. Most recovery after knee surgery happens at home without anyone watching, so the system has to run reliably there.

For my thesis I built Noban, an always-on voice assistant for older adults who live alone, and I took it from an idea to a working device. It runs on a Raspberry Pi with a MEMS microphone over I2S, an OLED display, status LEDs and Bluetooth Low Energy pairing. The whole pipeline is written in C++ with TensorFlow Lite and runs as system services, with a watchdog and automatic recovery so it keeps working without anyone touching it. A server and a Flutter app for caregivers handle alerts, pill reminders and calls. The device also runs a camera-based fall detector.

The machine learning part was a 75k-parameter wake-word model trained with int8 quantization-aware training, which runs in real time on the device. Under five-fold cross-validation it beat DS-CNN, TC-ResNet8, BC-ResNet and DS-CNNs matched to its size and compute on every fold. The most useful lesson came from real use. The device microphone tripled false alarms even though the offline scores barely moved, so I recorded hard negatives on the device and retrained. False activations fell from 60 to 3 in a 388-hour test. I expect the same gap between lab data and daily life in movement data from an orthosis, and I know how to measure it and close it.

This work produced a system paper accepted at ICBME 2026 (IEEE), a compact-model paper submitted to ICEE 2027 and a journal paper submitted to IEEE Sensors Journal. I also work as an embedded and ML systems engineer. I write firmware and Linux services in C and C++, I build wireless links and data pipelines, and I am at home with hands-on electronics. My strongest side is software and signal processing, and I would bring that to the orthosis while I learn the PCB design your compact sensor electronics need.

I care about the clinical side of this project. My device was built for older adults and their caregivers, and it taught me that a system only matters if patients and staff really use it. I would be glad to run usability and clinical studies with patients and therapists. My German is at an intermediate level, and I can reach B1 within the first year as the position asks.

My CV, transcripts and code (github.com/MGE-GOAT) are attached. Thank you for considering my application.
