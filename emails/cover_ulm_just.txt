I am applying for the doctoral position on the wearable knee orthosis at the Institute of Biomedical Engineering. I completed my MSc in Biomedical Engineering at Sharif University of Technology in September 2026. That is the problem I worked on in my thesis, in a different setting.

For my thesis I built Noban, an always-on voice assistant for older adults who live alone. It runs on a Raspberry Pi with a MEMS microphone, a small display and status LEDs. I built the device software in C++ with TensorFlow Lite and ran it as Linux services that restart on their own. A server and a Flutter app for caregivers handle alerts, pill reminders and calls. I tested the device in continuous use for five weeks, not only on a dataset.

I should say clearly what I do not know yet. I have not worked with ROS2, EMG or motion sensors, motor control or gait data. I have not designed a PCB or written firmware for a microcontroller. I have not run a study with patients. My experience is in signal processing, machine learning and building a complete device in software on a Raspberry Pi.

I am motivated to close these gaps, and I learn fast by building. Noban started as an idea, and I taught myself the parts I did not know, from the C++ audio pipeline to the server, the mobile app and the calls between the device and the phone. I keep several open-source projects with tests on GitHub, and at work I learned to build tool-using LLM agents within a few months. I love learning how things work by building them, and I want to go deeper into embedded systems and hardware design. A project that joins sensors, electronics, a motor and clinical use is the best place I can think of to do that. I would start with the electronics and the ROS2 system of the orthosis, and I would ask for help early.
