I am applying for the doctoral position on the wearable knee orthosis at the Institute of Biomedical Engineering. I completed my MSc in Biomedical Engineering at Sharif University of Technology in September 2026. Most recovery after knee surgery happens at home without anyone watching, and your project aims to build a device that works there. That is the problem I worked on in my thesis, in a different setting.

For my thesis I built Noban, an always-on voice assistant for older adults who live alone. It runs on a Raspberry Pi with a MEMS microphone, a small display and status LEDs. I built the device software in C++ with TensorFlow Lite and ran it as Linux services that restart on their own. A server and a Flutter app for caregivers handle alerts, pill reminders and calls. I tested the device in continuous use for five weeks, not only on a dataset.

The main thing I learned is that a model that looks good offline can behave differently on the sensor it really runs on. My thesis measured that gap for microphones, and retraining on data recorded on the device cut its false activations from 60 to 3 in a long stress test. I have not done this with movement or EMG data. I think the same way of working carries over, but I would have to show it, and I would start by checking it on your own recordings.

I should say clearly what I do not know yet. I have not worked with ROS2, EMG or motion sensors, motor control or gait data. I have not designed a PCB or written firmware for a microcontroller. I have not run a study with patients. My experience is in signal processing, machine learning and building a complete device in software on a Raspberry Pi. I would learn the rest in your group, starting with the electronics and the ROS2 system of the orthosis.

My thesis led to three papers. One is accepted at ICBME 2026, one is submitted to the IEEE Sensors Journal and one to ICEE 2027. I also work as an applied AI scientist writing production Python.
